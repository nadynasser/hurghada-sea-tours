Snorkeling for Non Swimmers in Hurghada: Safe Guide (2026)

✍️ Written by Hurghada Sea Tours Dive Team • ⏱️ 5 min read • 📍 Red Sea Marine Sanctuary • 📅 Scheduled Release: Thursday, Dec 4, 2026

One of the most common misconceptions among travelers visiting Egypt is that you must be an Olympic-level swimmer to experience the Red Sea's coral reefs. The reality is quite the opposite: snorkeling for non swimmers Hurghada is not only completely safe, but it is also one of our most popular and rewarding daily experiences.

Thanks to high-buoyancy certified life jackets, personal guidance from licensed divemasters, and the unusually high salinity of the Red Sea (which makes your body naturally buoyant), non-swimmers and complete beginners can float peacefully above colorful reef gardens with zero swimming effort.

Why the Red Sea is Ideal for Non-Swimmers

The Red Sea possesses unique physical characteristics that make it significantly easier and safer for first-timers compared to oceans in other parts of the world:

  • High Salinity & Natural Buoyancy: Due to high evaporation and limited river run-off, the Red Sea has a salt concentration of around 4%, making the human body float with virtually no treading water.
  • Incredible Water Visibility: Crystal-clear water with 25 to 35 meters of horizontal visibility means you can see every coral pinnacle and tropical fish directly from the surface without diving down.
  • Calm Island Lagoons: Excursions to Giftun Island, Orange Bay, and protected reefs like Shaab El Erg anchor in sheltered lagoons protected from open-ocean currents.

🛟 Dedicated In-Water Guide & Flotation Rings

On all Hurghada Sea Tours boat trips, non-swimmers receive individual attention. Our certified snorkel guides enter the water holding buoyant safety rings. You simply hold onto the ring, look through your mask, and allow the guide to swim you gently across the reef while you breathe calmly.

Top Protected Snorkeling Spots for Beginners in Hurghada

Not all Red Sea reefs have the same conditions. When booking your excursion, we specifically guide non-swimmers and beginners to sheltered bays featuring sandy bottoms and minimal current:

  • Shaab El Erg Lagoon: Renowned for its calm turquoise horseshoe lagoon where dolphins frequently rest and swim, offering flat water with zero swell.
  • Magawish Island Sandbars: Gentle sloping sandy bottoms where colorful reef fish swim in waist-deep water, allowing you to stand up securely whenever you like.
  • Giftun Island Protected Coves: Shaded shallows teeming with blue-spotted rays, butterflyfish, and clownfish living right next to the moored boats.

Essential Snorkeling Gear Provided for Beginners

You do not need to purchase expensive equipment beforehand. Our yachts are equipped with sanitized, professional-grade snorkeling gear:

  • Dry-Top Snorkel Tubes: Equipped with a specialized splash-guard mechanism that automatically seals if a wave passes overhead, preventing water from entering your mouth.
  • High-Clarity Tempered Glass Masks: Hypoallergenic soft silicone skirts that comfortably suction to facial contours without irritating skin or leaking.
  • Certified Marine Buoyancy Vests: Adjustable high-visibility vests that keep you suspended comfortably with zero arm or leg exertion.

Step-by-Step: How Non-Swimmers Snorkel Safely

  1. Snug Life Vest Fitting: Prior to entering the water, the crew fits an adjustable coast-guard approved life jacket that securely holds your chest and shoulders above water.
  2. Dry-Land Mask Practice: You practice breathing slowly through the snorkel tube while still on the boat deck, ensuring your silicone mask has a watertight seal.
  3. Gentle Water Entry: Rather than jumping, you step down our yacht's wide stern swim ladder directly into the calm water alongside your guide.
  4. Relaxed Floating: Lay face down in a horizontal position. Keep your arms relaxed at your sides and gently flutter your fins only when you wish to move.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can non-swimmers go snorkeling in Hurghada safely?
Yes, absolutely! Modern high-buoyancy life jackets keep your upper body floating effortlessly on the surface without any swimming effort. Professional snorkeling guides swim alongside you holding safety rings.
Do I need to know how to tread water or dive down?
No. Snorkeling occurs entirely on the surface. With a mask and breathing tube, you float face-down while the high salinity of the Red Sea provides natural buoyancy.
